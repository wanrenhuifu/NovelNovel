# Outline, structure and continuity

Method, not policy. The project's writing requirements and synopsis are authoritative
for what this book is. This file covers how to plan it and how to audit it.

## Three levels

- **Book** — the throughline: who wants what, what stands in the way, what the ending
  costs. One sentence. If it needs two, the book has two books in it.
- **Volume or arc** — a self-contained movement with its own question and its own
  answer, ending in a state change that the next volume inherits.
- **Chapter** — one scene or a small cluster, one point of view, a change of state.
  See `novel-scene` for what makes a chapter work.

## Throughline and subplots

Every plot line, main or subordinate, needs three things: a question the reader can
state, a resolution, and a reason it is in *this* book rather than another.

- Keep the count small. Three to five concurrent lines is already a lot for a reader
  to hold.
- Label them A/B/C and check the chapter list: the A line should not vanish for more
  than a chapter or two, and every subplot should touch the A line at least twice —
  once around the midpoint, once before the end.
- A subplot that resolves without affecting the main line is a separate story. Connect
  it or cut it.

## Setup and payoff

Readers accept almost anything planted early and paid off late. They reject almost
anything paid off that was never planted.

- Plant in the ordinary course of a scene; do not flag it. A flagged plant is a
  spoiler.
- Track the list explicitly and re-read it before writing the payoff chapters.
- A payoff that depends on a detail from forty chapters ago needs a light reminder
  shortly before.
- The converse failure is a planted thing never paid off. Pay it off or remove the
  plant — a reader who notices the gun on the wall and never sees it fire loses trust.

## Chapter beat sheet

For each chapter, before writing it, decide:

- Whose point of view, where, and when.
- What that character wants in the chapter, concretely.
- What obstructs it.
- What changes by the end — state, knowledge, relationship.
- What the reader learns that they did not know.
- What question is left open.
- How it connects to the A line.

A chapter that cannot fill this out is usually two chapters, or none.

## Full-draft consistency audit

When reading across chapters, check these — this is where long drafts break:

- **Timeline** — elapsed days, seasons, ages, travel time between places. Scenes
  written out of order are where these errors enter.
- **Knowledge** — who knows what, and since when. The commonest plot hole is a
  character acting on information never given to them on the page.
- **Physical facts** — scars, injuries, handedness, what a character carries, the
  layout of a building, the weather.
- **Naming** — names, titles, place names and forms of address must not drift. Where
  the project records a fact, record it once and refer to it, rather than restating
  it differently each time.
- **Payoffs** — every plant accounted for.
- **Voice** — does each point-of-view character's narration stay distinguishable?
  Compare an early chapter against a late one side by side.

## Chinese web-novel conventions

Genre expectations, not rules. Apply them when the project's writing requirements or
the user asks for this mode; ignore them for literary fiction.

- **黄金三章** — readers decide whether to continue within the first three chapters.
  Those chapters must establish who the protagonist is and what they want, the premise
  that makes this story different, and a first concrete win or loss. Do not open with
  worldbuilding — deliver the setting through a character acting inside it.
- **爽点** — the satisfaction loop is setup → competent action → payoff → escalation.
  Each instance should be legible within a few hundred words. Density matters more
  than size: a chapter with no beat loses readers faster than a chapter with a small
  one.
- **升级曲线** — growth in power, status or capability must be visible and stepped.
  The reader needs a ladder to watch the protagonist climb, and a sense of what the
  next rung costs.
- **追读** — the metric that matters is whether the reader starts the next chapter.
  Everything above serves it; chapter-end hooks (see `novel-scene`) are the mechanism.
- **断章** — end at maximum tension. The conventional place is just before the
  resolution, not after it.
- **Setting delivery** — whatever the premise's special mechanic is, the reader must
  understand its rules well enough to feel that the protagonist's choices matter.
  Rules stated once, plainly, early, beat rules dribbled out as mystification.
