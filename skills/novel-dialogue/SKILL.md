# Dialogue

Method, not policy. The brief from `novel_context` — writing requirements, active
preset, character cards — is authoritative for how these people speak. This file
says how to build a conversation; the brief says what it should sound like.

## What a line is for

A line should do at least two of: reveal character, advance the conflict, conceal
something, change the balance of the scene. A line that only delivers information
is exposition wearing a voice.

- **Subtext.** Characters rarely say what they mean. The gap between the line and
  the intent is where the scene lives. Write the gap, not the statement.
- **Conflict.** Two people who agree, and say so, end the scene. Even allies in a
  friendly scene want different things from the next five minutes.
- **Concealment.** What a character refuses to answer, answers evasively, or answers
  too quickly characterises them more sharply than anything they say plainly.

## Telling speakers apart

The test: strip every tag and attribution. Can you still tell who is speaking?

Build the distinction from consistent properties, not accents sprinkled on top:

- **Register** — education, class, era, profession; the words this person would
  never use.
- **Length** — one speaker answers in fragments, another in full sentences with the
  main clause last.
- **Habit** — a verbal tic, a repeated construction, how they open a sentence.
- **What they do with a question** — answer it, deflect it, answer a different one,
  or ask one back. The strongest and least mechanical discriminator.
- **Formality under pressure** — who gets more polite when angry, who gets cruder.

Give each major character at most *one* strong marker. Two markers per person in a
four-person scene reads as a costume.

## Tags and beats

- Prefer the plain said-family (说, 问, 答). A tag's job is attribution, not
  performance.
- Never carry emotion in the tag ("他愤怒地喊道"). Put it in the line or in a beat.
- **Use action beats instead of tags** wherever possible: a gesture, a pause,
  something done with an object. The beat attributes the line *and* keeps the scene
  physical. Do not stack a beat and a tag on one line unless the pause matters.
- With two speakers alternating, drop the tags for a stretch; the reader will track it.
- Place the tag where the sentence needs the pause. Mid-line tags change the rhythm.

## Avoiding talking heads

A conversation with no bodies in it is a screenplay with the camera removed.

- Intersperse beats: where people are, what they are doing, what they notice.
- Let the setting intrude once or twice — a sound, a change in light, someone else
  entering. It resets the reader's sense of place.
- Give the conversation a physical trajectory: someone stands, moves to the window,
  sets down a cup. The blocking can carry the emotional arc so the lines need not.

## Rhythm and silence

- Interruption, overlap and trailing off are dialogue. So is a non-answer.
- A short exchange after a long one lands harder than the reverse.
- Silence is a line. Say who did not answer, and what the other person does about it.
- Do not resolve every exchange. Ending a conversation in mid-air is a valid chapter
  ending — see `novel-scene` for chapter-end hooks.

## Chinese-specific traps

- **翻译腔** — "哦，我的天", "你这个愚蠢的家伙", nominalisation, and clauses in
  English word order. Read the line as Chinese speech, not as a translation.
- **Register bleeding** — every character using the same 口语 particles (啊, 吧, 呢)
  at the same rate. Particle density is itself a character marker; vary it on purpose.
- **Written-style dialogue** — characters speaking in complete, clause-balanced
  书面语. Real speech has false starts, ellipsis and dropped subjects.
- **Punctuation and placement** — follow the project's existing chapters for
  quotation style, tag placement and punctuation. Consistency within the book beats
  any house rule.
- **Forms of address** — 你/您, name versus title versus nickname versus nothing, and
  the moment a form shifts mid-scene. This carries relationship, rank and era more
  cheaply than any other signal, and it must stay constant unless the relationship
  changes on the page.
